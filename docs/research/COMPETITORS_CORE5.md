# Competitor Teardown: Noovit Core 5

Research date: **2026-10-29** (US). **V** means verified: I saw the price on the brand's own page or its live storefront catalog (`/products/*.js`) on 2026-10-29. **U** means unverified: the price comes from a third-party retailer or a search snippet. Amazon blocked automated fetches (HTTP 503), and so did Sports Research (HTTP 429), so **no Amazon prices are included**. Check those by hand before you quote them. Life Extension, Qualia and Kaneka-branded SKUs were not checked.

**$/day** is the one-time price divided by servings at the label serving. **Sub** is the subscription price. When a brand states the subscription discount only as a percentage, I give the percentage and not a computed price.

**Vitasy note.** vitasysupplements.com sells the same Supliful formulas under its **Cortex** (focus), **Fernwood** (rest), **Kinetic** (sport) and **Compass** sub-brands, plus a "vera" tag. Every product shows an inflated "Regular price" that is about 40% higher, a 20% Subscribe & Save, and a "Halloween Sale" banner. Its creatine listing says 250 g / 50 servings, but its own packshot reads "Net Wt 9.9 oz (281 g)". That is the same tub as ours.

## Overall: 7 label principles to beat Thorne, Momentous, Pure and Cortex at thumbnail size

Google Shopping tiles are about 100–180 px wide. At that size only two or three elements survive: one big shape, one number and one colour.

1. **One hero number, set huge.** Use a heavy weight at about 18–22% of label height, for example **275 mg**, **600 mg**, **5 g**, **200 mg**. Thorne, Momentous, Fernwood, Cortex and Kinetic show *no dose* on the front. This is the single biggest gap.
2. **Unit and form on line two.** Examples: "elemental Mg · from 2,500 mg bisglycinate" and "KSM-66 · 5% withanolides". This is the nerdy proof Nutricost and Double Wood show, but neither does it elegantly.
3. **Put the white bottle on an off-white label with one saturated colour block per SKU.** The block should cover about 25% of the label, as a top band or side bar, so the SKU reads by colour in a grid (Thorne does this with art). On white HDPE, a pure-white label vanishes against a white Shopping background.
4. **Keep the wordmark small and fixed in one spot, top left.** The product name goes in a medium-weight grotesk or mono. Momentous and Thorne prove that a small logo reads as confident. Avoid serif-plus-icon "apothecary" (Fernwood) and dark charcoal wraps (Cortex), both of which already belong to the Vitasy twins.
5. **Use an evidence-grade badge as the only seal.** Show a small grade (A/B/C) or "n trials" mark plus a "COA per batch" QR in one corner, and nothing else. No starbursts, "Best Seller" or "#1" banners (Doctor's Best, Qunol).
6. **Use a data strip at the bottom: "30 servings · 3 caps · $0.90/day".** Nutricost's stat row is the most legible thumbnail element in the whole set, but nobody premium uses it. Showing $/day on pack (or in the PDP image) turns price into a nerd feature.
7. **Hold to three type sizes and a 40%+ empty field.** If the label works in 1-bit black and white at 120 px, it will beat busy shelves (Host Defense, Jarrow, Qunol) and out-contrast Thorne's fine type.

## Addendum: what the owner's Google Shopping screenshots show (lion's mane, ashwagandha KSM-66)

Checked against the phone screenshots of the sponsored Shopping rows (Denver CO, Oct 2026). These override the
"off-white label" advice above where they disagree.

- **The Supliful white bottle is everywhere.** Cortex, Fernwood, Compass, Brainstorm and Revive all sell on the
  same bottle, many with the same two beige capsules in the bottom-right of the photo. Side by side they read as
  one product; white or off-white labels (Fernwood, Revive, Thorne) melt into the white tile.
- **What wins the eye at tile size (~120–140 px):** saturated colour over most of the pack (Vitasy gummies, Goli,
  Physician's Choice, Tribe Organics, Eversio), dark glass (Superfeast, 3rd Planet, Innerbody) and big numbers
  (Physician's Choice "1000 mg", Revive "KSM-66"). Almost nobody shows the dose large, so a giant dose is ownable.
- **Competitors fill the tile.** Their images are cropped so the label spans most of the tile width; our mockups
  showed the bottle at under half the width. Tight framing is the single biggest legibility win.
- **Revive already owns a vertical spine wordmark** on a white bottle, so the spine direction (F) is not distinctive.
- **Dark navy/charcoal wraps = Vitasy/Compass look.** Our creatine navy sits close to it; consider a brighter colour.
- **Light tinted backgrounds are used** (Vitasy pale blue, Bettervits mint) and stand out from pure white tiles.

Label exploration renders (scratchpad, not committed): A current, E spec band, F spec spine, G colour hero.
G (product colour over ~70% of the label, white wordmark top-left, giant white dose, paper strip below for form and
data) framed tight is the clear winner in a mock Shopping row next to the real competitors.

## Decision (29 Oct) and FourFive reference

- **Chosen:** "A": the colour block stays (category, name, vertical wordmark); the white band carries the dose per
  serving on the left and the net quantity on the right at legal size. The owner found G's giant dose too loud.
- **Doses vs. the market:** magnesium 275 mg elemental (Thorne 120, Momentous 200) and CoQ10 200 mg (Thorne and
  Double Wood 100) are above average; ashwagandha 600 mg KSM-66 is the studied dose; lion's mane 1,000 mg and
  creatine 5 g are standard. The dose goes on every single-ingredient front for consistency; comparisons
  ("2× Thorne's dose") belong on the site and in ads, not on the pack.
- **FourFive (owner's reference, H&B lion's mane gummies):** one saturated colour over the whole label, a big
  horizontal wordmark that is part of the layout, a pill-shaped benefit tag, a thin line illustration, and key facts
  as a short ruled list. Gallery images do the heavy lifting: the bottle on a dark gummy texture with three ingredient
  callouts. Worth borrowing for our gallery images 2–4. Their "4,000 mg" is a mushroom-equivalent figure from an
  extract; our honest 1,000 mg needs a one-line explainer on the product page.
