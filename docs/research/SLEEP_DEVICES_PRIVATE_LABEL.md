# Private-label sleep and snoring products, US OTC (research, 19 Nov 2026)

Web research only; no supplier contacted. FDA records were checked on 19 Nov 2026 in the FDA 510(k) database, summary
PDFs and openFDA (registration/listing and device-ID mirrors, weekly). The FDA site rate-limited some lookups, so a few
came via openFDA or innolitics.com (a mirror of FDA data). MOQs and prices are unconfirmed unless a source is given.

## Key points
- **Anti-snoring mouthpieces are Class II** (21 CFR 872.5570, codes LRK/LQZ). Each needs a 510(k). OTC clearances cover
  **only "aid in the reduction of snoring" in adults 18+**, never sleep apnea.
- **Selling under someone else's clearance is allowed:** a distributor that adds only its name and changes no other
  labeling needs no 510(k) ([21 CFR 807.85(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-807.85)). It's done today:
  SnoreLessNow sells Oscimed's device, REAZEAL sells Dongguan Yiyao's.
- **The clearance holder is often not the factory.** The right to sell comes from the **holder**.
- **Nasal strips and nasal vents: Class I, 510(k)-exempt** (21 CFR 874.3900, LWF; the GMP rules still apply). The
  regulation covers breathing and congestion; snoring isn't in the text. Whether "reduces snoring" stays exempt needs a
  regulatory consultant.
- **Mouth tape and chin straps have no specific FDA classification.** Sellers making snoring claims list them under
  Class I exempt codes (KGX tape, LWF, MDR binder). Our earlier review found weak evidence and a suffocation risk for
  mouth tape (see `NEW_PRODUCT_LINES.md`).

Rules for OTC mouthpieces: FDA [special controls guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/intraoral-devices-snoring-andor-obstructive-sleep-apnea-class-ii-special-controls-guidance-document)
§9 labeling.
- **Contraindications:** central sleep apnea, severe breathing disorders, loose teeth or advanced gum disease, under 18.
- **Warnings:** tooth movement or bite changes, gum or tooth soreness, jaw-joint pain, blocked mouth breathing, excess saliva.
- **Boil-and-bite devices:** heat, cool and set times.

## What a private-label distributor of a 510(k)-cleared device must do
1. **No new 510(k)** if only the name changes ([807.85(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-807.85)). Any change to the indication (e.g. sleep apnea) or the design needs one.
2. **Label:** "Distributed by Noovit LLC" plus street address ([801.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-801.1)).
3. **Registration** ([807.20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-807.20), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-registration-and-listing/who-must-register-list-and-pay-fee)):
   - A distributor that doesn't relabel: none.
   - **An initial importer, relabeler or specification developer must register and pay: FY 2027 fee $13,785** ([Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15335.pdf)).
   - **Buying from a US company avoids becoming the importer.**
4. **Complaint records** ([803.18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-803.18)). Importers report deaths or serious injuries within 30 days ([803.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-803.40)). The supply agreement should send complaints to the clearance holder.
5. **Device ID (UDI)** for Class II, held by whoever causes the label to be applied ([801.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-801.3)). For Class I, a UPC suffices.
6. **Quality system (21 CFR 820 / ISO 13485)** binds the manufacturer, not a pure distributor.
7. **Claims:** "FDA-cleared" (K number), never "FDA-approved". Snoring only, 18+, never sleep apnea.

## Recommendation
Low priority versus the categories in `NEW_CATEGORIES_WHITE_LABEL.md`. If pursued:
1. **SmartGuard Rx:** a US supplier, so no importer registration or fee.
2. **Oscimed SA:** proven private-label route.

Fallbacks: Dongguan Yiyao (budget), Jiangsu Yizheng Carepro (strips plus tape). Ask every supplier for:
- MOQ and unit price
- a **written right to use their 510(k)**
- who is the device-ID labeler
- who handles complaints and adverse-event reports
- duty-paid delivery to a US address

A home sleep apnea test referral (Lofta-type) pairs naturally with any snoring product: it sends people who may have
apnea to a doctor.
