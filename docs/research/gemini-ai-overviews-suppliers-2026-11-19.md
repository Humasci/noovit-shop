Turnkey white-label platforms like IHDLab or imaware do not exist in the same plug-and-play format for pets, because veterinary diagnostics and human CLIA labs operate under separate regulatory systems. Instead, US pet supplement brands build "test + supplement" ecosystems by combining B2B diagnostic lab partnerships (or OEM rapid test kits) with private-label pet supplement manufacturers and pet wearables/telehealth.

1. At-Home Pet Lab & Kit Partners (USA)

Depending on the type of test you want to pair with your supplements, you will work with specialized veterinary labs or OEM rapid-test manufacturers:
• Gut Microbiome & Stool Biomarkers: Innovative Pet Lab (Georgia) and AnimalBiome (California) are the leading US labs for at-home feline and canine stool testing, measuring gut inflammation, leaky gut, parasites, and microbiome diversity. Both work with B2B partners and retail brands to pair gut panels with digestive supplements, probiotics, and food toppers.
• Genetic Health & Breed Panels: Orivet offers B2B partner programs for dog and cat cheek-swab DNA kits, screening over 414 genetic health conditions, traits, and nutritional insights.

• Food & Environmental Sensitivities: Non-invasive hair and saliva biofeedback test providers like Glacier Peak Holistics screen 395+ food and environmental stressors without a vet blood draw, making them popular companion kits for skin, coat, and allergy chew brands. (Note: True serum IgE allergy tests like Antech Allercept require a licensed veterinarian to draw blood).

2. Expanding Into "More" for Pet Brands

• Private-Label Pet Supplements: If you are expanding from human supplements into pets, US contract manufacturers like Bimini Pet Health, Pet Tech Labs, Zero Tolerance Labs, and PetDine produce NASC-audited soft chews, powders, and toppers (digestion, mobility, calming, skin/coat, urinary) with low minimums starting around 500 units.
• Connected Hardware & Smart Monitoring: Brands often pair consumables with smart collars or litter monitors (such as Purina's Petivity ecosystem or GPS/activity trackers) to track changes in scratching, digestion, or mobility after starting a supplement.
• Virtual Vet Triage: Partnering with a veterinary telehealth network allows pet parents to review abnormal stool or urine results with a licensed vet without requiring your brand to employ clinicians.

3. Key Regulatory Differences from Human Testing

• No CLIA Requirement, Strict FDA/CVM & NASC Rules: Animal diagnostic labs do not fall under human CLIA regulations, which lowers lab-integration friction. However, the FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM) strictly prohibits pet supplements and non-vet test kits from claiming to diagnose, cure, or treat diseases. Frame your tests around "gut balance," "wellness screening," and "nutritional support."
• Sample Collection Reality: Pet owners strongly prefer stool, cheek swab, hair/saliva, or litter-box urine collection over at-home blood draws. Avoid finger-prick or lancet-style kits for pets.
