# Methylene blue drops: Google Shopping + go/no-go (US, 28 Oct 2026)

_Source: owner's 6 screenshots of the Sponsored Products carousel for "methylene blue drops" (Denver, CO), 16 listings, plus the Supliful product page. Full table, tiers, profit and per-mg comparison: `docs/pricing/noovit-pricing.xlsx` (Methylene Blue tabs)._

## Recommendation: **don't sell it now.** Write the guide instead.
The demand is real (AccuaBoost has 9k+ reviews at 4.9), and the price per mg would be fine. But three problems come before price, and each one alone is enough to wait:

### 1. It may not be a legal dietary supplement
- Methylene blue is an **FDA-approved prescription drug** (injectable, for methemoglobinemia) and is listed by the FDA as a prescription generic. Under US law, an ingredient approved as a drug generally **can't be sold as a dietary supplement** unless it was marketed as a supplement or food first. It also doesn't fit the usual dietary-ingredient categories (vitamin, mineral, herb, amino acid…).
- NPR (Dec 2025) reported that it's **unclear whether methylene blue meets the definition of a supplement**, that most products aren't in the federal supplement label database, and that the FDA didn't answer how it's sold without a prescription.
- The grid shows it: **3 of 16 listings say "FOR RESEARCH USE ONLY"** (PeakForce, Mile High, BioEdge), a grey-market workaround, and one (algorx) uses a pharmacy-style Rx bottle.
- For a brand built on "evidence-graded" and trust, the downside (FDA warning letter, payment processor or Merchant Center suspension, insurer refusing cover) is far bigger than one product's profit. **This needs the regulatory lawyer's answer first.**

### 2. A serious interaction affects a big share of buyers
- Methylene blue **inhibits MAO-A**. The FDA warned in 2011 that it can cause **serotonin syndrome** with SSRIs, SNRIs and clomipramine. The reported cases were mostly IV doses of 1–8 mg/kg; the FDA said the risk at oral or lower doses is unknown.
- Antidepressants are among the most commonly used medicines in the US, and our audience (biohackers, focus, stress) overlaps heavily with people on SSRIs.
- It's dangerous for people with **G6PD deficiency** (it can trigger haemolysis), it isn't for pregnancy, and it turns urine blue-green.
- **Supliful's label warning doesn't mention SSRIs, MAOIs or G6PD.** It only says "the safety of this product has not been determined". We'd have to write our own much stronger warnings, and even then a buyer on an SSRI could be hurt.

### 3. The evidence doesn't support the dose
- The best-known human study (Rodriguez et al., *Radiology* 2016) used **one dose of about 280 mg** (≈4 mg/kg) and found a small short-term memory effect in 26 healthy adults.
- Supliful's product is **10 mg a day**, about 1/28 of that. On our grading scale this would be **"insufficient evidence"** at the sold dose, which undercuts the brand.

## What to do instead
1. **Write the guide:** "Methylene blue: what the evidence shows, the dose problem, and who must avoid it (SSRIs, G6PD)". Search interest is high and most pages are sellers. An honest, cited page fits our content plan and can rank, earn links and AI-overview mentions.
2. In the guide, point to **evidence-graded alternatives** for focus and energy that we can sell cleanly: lion's mane, L-theanine (+ caffeine), creatine, and later citicoline.
3. **Ask the regulatory lawyer** (already on the TODO) about methylene blue's status. Revisit only if the answer is a clear yes and the label carries the full SSRI/MAOI/G6PD warnings.
