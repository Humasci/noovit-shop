## Printable starter kit (PDF, our style, 4 pages)
- **Page 1, "What's happening":** stages (STRAW+10 in plain words) and how hormones swing [M1–2].
- **Page 2, "4-week tracker":** a daily grid for sleep, hot flashes/night sweats, mood, energy, period/bleeding, and notes.
- **Page 3, "What helps", evidence-graded table** [W summary table]:
  - CBT-I for sleep (Moderate); CBT or clinical hypnosis for hot flashes (Moderate).
  - Strength training for muscle (Moderate) and bone (Limited).
  - Caffeine timing and alcohol for sleep (Moderate).
  - Cooling and avoiding triggers: comfort steps, not tested as treatment (Insufficient as treatment).
  - Herbal remedies for hot flashes (Insufficient to Limited; not recommended by The Menopause Society).
  - **No brand names on this page.**
- **Page 4, "Talk to your doctor":**
  - questions to ask
  - options doctors can offer: hormone therapy, non-hormone prescriptions, CBT/hypnosis [M5]
  - **red flags:** bleeding after 12 months without a period, bleeding between periods or after sex, much heavier bleeding, severe low mood (988) [M6].

## Six weekly emails
1. **Week 1: "What perimenopause really is (and isn't)".**
   - Content: the transition, its timeline and stages [M1]; symptoms vary, and up to 80% get hot flashes [M3]; the kit attached; start the tracker.
   - Action: track 7 days.
   - No products.
2. **Week 2: "Your hormones, simply".**
   - Content: estrogen and progesterone swing, they don't fall steadily [M2]; why one blood test is often not useful after 45, and when testing does matter (under 45) [M2].
   - Action: note cycle changes.
   - No products. One line: "We're building an at-home Perimenopause Check; you'll hear first."
3. **Week 3: "Sleep, night sweats and a busy mind".**
   - Content: CBT-I (84% vs 43% insomnia-free in one trial, Moderate) [W1]; caffeine timing (last coffee ~9 h before bed, Moderate); alcohol fragments sleep (Moderate) [W5].
   - Soft mention: "Studies of magnesium and sleep are small and low quality [W6, Limited]. If you want to try it, our Magnesium Sleep+ is 275 mg per serving; stay at or under 350 mg/day from supplements."
4. **Week 4: "Hot flashes: what actually works".**
   - Content: hormone therapy is the most effective option and benefit-risk is favorable for most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause [M5]; non-hormone prescriptions [M5]; CBT and clinical hypnosis (Level I) [W2]; weight loss may help (Limited); cooling and layers are comfort steps (not tested as treatment); herbal remedies are mixed and not recommended by The Menopause Society [W9].
   - **No product mention** (see the note below).
5. **Week 5: "Strength, bones and energy".**
   - Content: fastest bone loss from 1 year before to 2 years after the last period (~2.5%/yr at the spine) [M4]; strength training builds muscle (Moderate) and, under supervision, improved spine density in one trial (Limited) [W3]; protein (consensus figure for over-65s only) [W4]; vitamin D/calcium basics, and that supplements didn't cut fractures in people who weren't low (VITAL) [W8]; iron and heavy periods: get checked first [W10].
   - Soft mention: "Creatine with strength training adds strength and lean mass (Moderate in older adults); it didn't change bone density in a 2-year trial [W7]. Our Creatine for Her is 5 g per scoop."
6. **Week 6: "Mood, brain fog and your doctor's appointment".**
   - Content: higher-risk window for low mood, especially with a past history (988 in a crisis) [M3]; memory dips are common and tend to recover after menopause [M3]; how to use your tracker at the appointment; questions to ask [M7]; red flags [M6].
   - Soft mention: the Perimenopause Check waitlist, plus a link to our guides.
   - Close: "Reply and tell us what helped."

## Important note on Menopause Balance+
- Our Menopause Balance+ (black cohosh, sage, D3, B6) claims "supports comfort during hot flashes*".
- The research says black cohosh is Insufficient and sage is Early, and The Menopause Society does not recommend supplements for hot flashes.
- An evidence-led program that recommends it for hot flashes would contradict itself. So:
  1. Leave it out of the program.
  2. Review the product's claim wording (still legal as structure/function, but it doesn't match our standard). Or keep it hidden until we have a better formula.
- The owner decides.
