## Notes on the four new ones

### NMN (30 capsules, 500 mg each, 1 a day)
- **Legal status:** the FDA said in 2022 that NMN couldn't be sold as a supplement (a drug company was studying it). After a citizen petition and a lawsuit by the Natural Products Association, **the FDA reversed this on 29 Sept 2025**, confirming NMN is lawful in dietary supplements because it was sold as one before the drug research started. (Amazon's own rules may still lag; check before listing there.) **This replaces the "exclude NMN" note in `SUPPLIER_RESEARCH.md`.**
- **Evidence:** NMN reliably raises blood NAD+ in human trials (250–1,200 mg/day, well tolerated). Functional benefits are small and mixed (e.g. insulin sensitivity in a small trial of women with prediabetes at 250 mg/day; walking distance in older adults). That's an honest **evidence grade C**.
- **Compared with NAD+:** NMN is the better product of the two, and if we do a "healthy ageing" product it should be NMN or NR, not oral NAD+.
- **Watch-outs:** a 30-capsule bottle means a monthly reorder (good for subscriptions); price unknown; NMN is often counterfeit or under-dosed in the market, so ask for a purity COA per lot (HPLC).
- **Claims:** "supports NAD+ production" is fine. Avoid "longevity", "anti-aging" and "reverses aging".

### Colostrum (60 capsules, 500 mg, 25% IgG, 1–3 a day)
- **Why it's interesting:** colostrum is one of the hottest gut and immune trends (ARMRA and others on social), and it suits our **Gut & Immunity Reset** bundle.
- **Dose problem:** human studies on gut permeability and immunity mostly used **1–20 g a day**; one capsule is 0.5 g, and three a day is 1.5 g. That's the low end, and a powder would hit study doses far more easily.
- **Label must-fix:** colostrum *is* milk. The label needs **"Contains: milk"** (Supliful's page only says the facility "may also process milk"). Not vegan, not dairy-free.
- **Other:** L-leucine has no amount listed; ask for the full Supplement Facts.
- **Verdict:** a possible test product or bundle component. Better long-term: a colostrum **powder** at a study dose.

### Omega-3 (100 softgels, EPA 180 + DHA 120 mg each)
- This is the classic **1,000 mg fish oil at 30% concentration**, the spec of mass-market fish oils. Two softgels give 600 mg EPA+DHA a day, which meets general guidance (250–500 mg for healthy adults) but is weak next to premium oils (about 1,000–1,300 mg per serving).
- **What's missing for a premium brand:** the form (triglyceride vs ethyl ester), the fish source, and **third-party freshness testing** (IFOS or a TOTOX oxidation value). Freshness is the quality signal in this category.
- **Gelatin softgel**, source not stated, so not vegetarian; it may not suit pescatarians either.
- **Verdict:** fine as a cheap basic or bundle component. For a hero omega-3, source a concentrated, IFOS-certified triglyceride oil (or an algae oil for vegans). Ask Supliful whether they have one.

### Vitamin D3 2,000 IU (100 softgels)
- A sensible, standard dose (50 mcg; the upper limit is 4,000 IU). Carrier is soybean and corn oil in a gelatin softgel, so not vegetarian.
- **Commodity market:** big brands sell 2,000 IU softgels for around $10, so a standalone premium D3 makes little sense on a ~$10 landed cost.
- **Supliful claims "increases energy levels"**, which isn't supported unless someone is deficient. Drop it.
- Our catalog already plans **Vitamin D3 & K2** (2,000 IU + 75 mcg MK-7), a better hero. Use plain D3 only as an add-on or in bundles (GLP-1 companion, Women 40+).

### Ashwagandha Plus (60 capsules, 2 a day), added 28 Oct
- **The good part is excellent:** **KSM-66** is the best-known branded ashwagandha extract. **600 mg a day** (5% withanolides) is the dose used in the main human trials on stress, cortisol and sleep. A branded, studied ingredient on the label is exactly what an evidence-graded brand wants. It also matches our planned **Ashwagandha Calm+** (600 mg, 5% withanolides).
- **The extras are "label dressing":** L-arginine 300 mg (studies use 3–6 g), maca 150 mg (studies use 1.5–3 g), Panax ginseng 100 mg as plain powder (studies use 200–400 mg of extract), shatavari 50 mg. They're too small to grade, and stimulating ginseng and arginine sit oddly with a *calm and sleep* product. The vitamins (D3 800 IU, B6, B12) are low and harmless.
- **Best option:** ask Supliful for a **plain KSM-66 600 mg** capsule and sell that as Ashwagandha Calm+, saying "KSM-66, 600 mg, the dose used in the studies". If they don't have one, this product works, but we'd say honestly that KSM-66 does the work. It could also become a separate "energy & vitality" product.
- **Warnings needed** (Supliful's are generic): not in pregnancy or breastfeeding; talk to a doctor with thyroid conditions (ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone), autoimmune disease, liver problems (rare liver-injury reports), or if taking sedatives, thyroid, diabetes or blood-pressure medication. Ginseng can interact with blood thinners. Some European countries have restricted ashwagandha, which is worth a line in our guide.
- **Licensing:** using the KSM-66 name and logo needs the brand owner's (Ixoreal) approval, usually arranged through the supplier. Ask Supliful.
- **Market check (done):** see `SHOPPING_ANALYSIS_ASHWAGANDHA.md`. Plain capsules sell for $22–39 (median ~$30). **Vitasy sells this exact product as Cortex, Compass and Fernwood Ashwagandha Plus at $29.99**, so if we use it, price at $30–32 / $27 subscribed, or better, get a plain KSM-66.

### Reishi Relax gummies (30 gummies, 1–2 a day), added 28 Oct
- Reishi extract 200 mg (no ratio, beta-glucan figure or fruiting-body statement) + **25 mg each** of L-theanine, lemon balm, passion flower and valerian. Studies use about 200 mg L-theanine and 300–600 mg valerian or lemon balm, so these are label dressing.
- Human evidence for reishi and relaxation or sleep is weak. Organic cane sugar and tapioca syrup; pectin (vegan).
- A 30-gummy bottle at 1–2 a day lasts only 15–30 days.
- **Verdict: no.** If we want a calm gummy later, L-theanine at 200 mg is the evidence-backed option.

### Cordyceps Energy gummies (30 gummies, 1–2 a day), added 28 Oct
- **Cordyceps extract 200 mg**, with no species (militaris or CS-4), ratio, fruiting body or cordycepin figure. The page's own evidence section cites a different extract (CordyMax Cs-4) at **1 g a day**, with only non-significant trends. Studies generally use 1–3 g a day.
- **Add-ons at 25 mg each** are label dressing: GABA (barely crosses into the brain when swallowed), ginkgo (studies use 120–240 mg), L-glutamine (studies use grams) and DMAE (weak evidence).
- Ginkgo can add to blood thinners, which needs a warning even at low doses. No caffeine, despite the "energy" name.
- Organic cane sugar and tapioca syrup; pectin (vegan). 30 gummies last 15–30 days.
- **Verdict: no.** Supliful's gummy line uses one template (≈200 mg mushroom + four 25 mg extras), so expect the same from its other mushroom gummies. If we want cordyceps later, look for a CS-4 or militaris fruiting-body extract at 1 g+ in capsules.

### Chaga (60 capsules, 2 a day), added 28 Oct
- **It's mycelium, not chaga as people know it.** Traditional chaga is the black conk (sclerotium) that grows on birch trees. This is lab-grown mycelium powder, and "40% polysaccharides" again counts grain starch.
- **Evidence:** almost entirely lab and animal studies. Supliful's page cites COVID (SARS-CoV-2) and blood-sugar research. We couldn't use either claim.
- **Safety:** chaga is very high in **oxalates**; there are case reports of kidney damage from long-term chaga use. It may add to diabetes medicines and blood thinners. Supliful's page has no such warnings.
- **Verdict: no.** (Our catalog's Mushroom Clarity+ powder blend also lists chaga 500 mg; review it when the real products arrive.)
