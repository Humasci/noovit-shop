**Short answer:** they do different jobs. **Glycinate** packs about twice as much magnesium per gram, so it's the practical way to top up your daily intake [1][2][3]. **Threonate** (sold as Magtein) is marketed for the brain. That idea comes mainly from rat studies and a handful of small human trials, most of them funded by companies behind the ingredient [4][5][6][7]. One independent trial found no benefit [8]. No study has compared the two forms head to head.

## Magnesium per gram

The label must list the **elemental magnesium**, the magnesium itself, not the weight of the whole compound [9]. This is where the two forms differ most.

| | Glycinate (bisglycinate) | L-threonate |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium by weight | About 14% [1] | About 7.5–8% [2][3] |
| Magnesium in 1 g of compound | About 140 mg [1] | About 75–83 mg [2][3] |
| Compound needed for 250 mg of magnesium | About 1.8 g [1] | About 3 g [3] |
| Typical study dose | 250 mg magnesium a day [1] | 1–2 g compound, so about 75–150 mg magnesium [2][7] |

Adult women need 310–320 mg of magnesium a day, and men 400–420 mg [9]. A typical threonate dose covers a smaller share of that than a typical glycinate dose.

## Where the brain claims come from

**The rat study.** In 2010, researchers reported that magnesium L-threonate, then a newly developed compound, raised brain magnesium in rats. The rats did better on learning and memory tasks, and had more synapses in a memory region of the brain [4]. This is the foundation of most threonate marketing. We found no published study that measured brain magnesium in people taking threonate.

**The human trials.** These are the randomized, placebo-controlled trials of threonate that measured memory or thinking, from our PubMed search:

| Trial | Who | Dose, length | Result | Funding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 [5] | 44 adults aged 50–70 whom the study classed as cognitively impaired | 1.5–2 g a day, 12 weeks | Better overall score on a composite of cognitive tests (effect size 0.91). Sleep and mood didn't differ from placebo | The company developing the compound; three authors worked for it |
| 2022 [6] | 109 healthy adults in China | 2 g a day of a formula (threonate plus phosphatidylserine and vitamins), 30 days | Better scores on all parts of a memory test | Wu-xi Magtein Bioscience |
| 2023 [8] | 109 people recovering from surgery | Threonate, 12 weeks | **No difference** in memory, mood or sleep scores | No conflicts declared |
| 2026 [7] | 100 adults aged 18–45 with poor sleep | 2 g a day, 6 weeks | Better overall score on the NIH cognition toolbox (p = 0.043). No difference on a reasoning test | Threotech, which supplied the product and helped design the study |

What this adds up to:

- **Small and short.** The largest trial had 109 people, and none ran longer than 12 weeks.
- **Mostly maker-funded.** Three of the four were funded by companies linked to the ingredient [5][6][7]. That doesn't make the results wrong, but independent replication matters, and the one independent trial was negative [8].
- **No comparison with other forms.** Every trial compared threonate with a placebo. So even where it helped, we can't tell whether threonate did better than the same amount of magnesium as glycinate or citrate would have.
- **One used a blend.** The 2022 formula also contained phosphatidylserine and vitamins, so the effect can't be credited to threonate alone [6].

Our grade for threonate and cognition: **Limited**.

## Sleep: what each has shown

**Glycinate.** In a 2025 trial, 155 adults with self-reported poor sleep took 250 mg of magnesium as bisglycinate, or a placebo, 30–60 minutes before bed for 4 weeks. Insomnia scores fell 3.9 points with magnesium versus 2.3 with placebo, a small effect. Other sleep and mood questionnaires didn't change [1]. It was funded by the university that ran it; a supplement company made the capsules [1]. Note that the daily dose also contained about 1.5 g of glycine [1].

**Threonate.** Three trials looked at sleep:

- **2024, 80 adults, 1 g a day for 3 weeks:** insomnia scores didn't differ from placebo. Ring-tracker deep and REM sleep scores, and some morning alertness and mood ratings, were better. Total sleep time and time to fall asleep didn't improve [2]. It was funded by AIDP, and three authors worked for that company [2].
- **2026, 100 adults, 2 g a day for 6 weeks:** one self-reported measure (sleep-related impairment) improved more than placebo. Two others didn't, and the sleep ring found no differences [7].
- **2016, 44 older adults:** sleep scores improved in both groups, with no difference from placebo [5].

So both forms have **Limited** evidence for sleep: small effects on some measures, no change on others. Neither has been shown to beat the other. More on timing in [when to take magnesium for sleep](/blog/when-to-take-magnesium-for-sleep).

## Cost per mg of magnesium

Because threonate holds less magnesium per gram, it costs more per milligram unless it's priced much lower. To compare any two products:

**Price ÷ (elemental magnesium per serving × servings) × 100 = cost per 100 mg of magnesium.**

As an example, take two bottles at the same price, each with 90 capsules holding 500 mg of compound. The bisglycinate bottle gives about 70 mg of magnesium per capsule, or about 6,300 mg in total [1]. The threonate bottle gives about 38–42 mg per capsule, or about 3,400–3,700 mg [2][3]. At the same price, you get a little over half the magnesium from threonate.

## Safety

The same rules apply to both:

- **Keep supplements at or below 350 mg of magnesium a day** unless your doctor advises more. Food doesn't count [9].
- **Diarrhea** is the most common side effect, mostly at higher doses [9]. Both forms were well tolerated in their trials [1][2].
- **Threonate:** Europe's food safety agency judged it safe for adults at up to 3,000 mg of compound a day (about 250 mg of magnesium). The use it assessed excluded pregnant and breastfeeding women [3].
- **Kidney disease:** don't take magnesium supplements unless your doctor prescribes them [9].
- **Medicines:** magnesium binds to tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics and to bisphosphonates, so space the doses. Long-term PPIs and some diuretics change magnesium levels [9].

## Which one to pick

- **You want to meet your daily magnesium need, or support sleep quality:** glycinate. More magnesium per capsule, the larger sleep trial, and usually lower cost per mg [1].
- **You're specifically interested in the cognition research:** threonate is the form that's been studied, but know the evidence is small and mostly funded by its makers [5][6][7][8].
- **You want both:** count the elemental magnesium from each and keep the total from supplements at or below 350 mg [9].
